Free Property Investigation for California Homeowners

Before recommending anything, we investigate. Ownership, value, mortgages, taxes, liens, heirs, code issues, and deadlines, so you understand what is actually happening with your property. We don't lead with an offer.

The investigation is for anyone with a California property and a question they cannot answer yet. The property taxes are behind and you do not know how far. A parent died and nobody is sure who owns the house now. Someone found a lien or a judgment you did not know about. Or you were trying to sell or refinance, and the title company or escrow officer said there is a problem with the title, and that is all anyone has told you.

You do not need to know what is wrong to ask. Finding out is the point. We are a private real estate solutions company, not a title company, a law firm, or a lender, and the investigation is free.

What the investigation covers

Ownership and how title is held

Who is on the recorded deed, how they hold it (individually, as joint tenants, as community property, in a trust), and whether the chain of ownership has gaps, errors, or a deceased owner still on record.

Value

An estimate of the property's current value, for planning only. It tells you roughly how much equity sits behind the debts and helps compare options. It is not an appraisal.

Mortgages and deeds of trust

Every recorded deed of trust, who holds it, and whether any old loans that were paid off were never formally released.

Property taxes

Current status with the county tax collector, any defaulted tax years, the amount needed to redeem, and whether the property is scheduled for a tax sale and when.

Liens

Tax liens, mechanics liens, homeowners association liens, and any other recorded claim against the property.

Judgments

Recorded abstracts of judgment against any owner, which attach to California real property in the county where they are recorded.

Heirs, if an owner has died

Whether any transfer was ever filed, who the heirs are under the will or under California's intestate succession rules, and which heirs still need to be located.

Code issues

Open code enforcement cases and permit problems on file with the city or county.

Deadlines

Every date that matters: tax sale dates, filing windows after a death, and any court or lender deadline that turns up in the records.

Where the information comes from

Public records: the county recorder (deeds, deeds of trust, liens, abstracts of judgment), the county assessor (assessed value and ownership filings), the county tax collector (tax status and redemption amounts), superior court records (probate and civil cases), and city or county code enforcement and permit records. Plus whatever you tell us and any documents you already have.

What the written summary looks like

You get a plain-language written summary, usually within about a week. It covers four things:

What we found. Who owns the property, what is recorded against it, and where the taxes stand.

What it means. Which findings are serious, which are routine, and which are paperwork.

The deadlines. Each date that matters, in order.

Every option. Keep, refinance, sell, pay off, clear, or restructure, including the options that do not involve us at all.

What happens after

We walk through the summary with you and answer your questions. No offer is attached. You decide what to do next, and many people take the summary and handle it themselves, with their own attorney, or through a regular sale through escrow.

If you choose a path that involves us, such as a structured sale or a direct purchase, we explain exactly how we would earn from it before you agree to anything. When a step needs an attorney, we bring one in.

What it costs

Nothing. The investigation and the written summary are free, with no obligation and no pressure to use us afterward.

Common questions

How long does the investigation take?

We respond within one business day. The written summary usually arrives within about a week. Properties with missing heirs or long ownership gaps can take longer, and we tell you if yours will.

What do I need to give you?

The property address and a description of the situation are enough to start. Anything you already have helps: tax bills, letters from the county, a notice from a lender, a will or trust, or whatever the title company or escrow sent you.

Can you investigate a property I don't own, like a relative's house?

Yes. Most of what we review is public record. It is common for a family member to ask about a parent's or sibling's house, or for an heir to ask about a property they may have inherited. What can be done next depends on who has legal authority over the property, and the summary explains that.

Is my information shared with anyone?

No. What you tell us stays between you and us. We do not sell your information, and we share it only with someone you approve, such as an attorney we bring in on your case.

Are you a title company?

No. We do not issue title insurance and the summary is not a title policy or a preliminary title report. Title insurance is issued by a title insurer, usually through a title company at escrow. Our job is to find out what is wrong, explain it, and help fix it, so the property can be insured, sold, or refinanced when you are ready.

Is this legal advice?

No. We are not a law firm and the summary is not legal advice. When your situation needs an attorney, we tell you and we bring one in.
