Behind on property taxes in Stanislaus County

Property taxes here are collected by the Stanislaus County Treasurer-Tax Collector, 1010 10th Street, Suite 2500, Modesto, CA 95354. The office is open Monday through Friday, 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m., and the property tax line is (209) 525-6388. You can look up and pay a bill through the county's online property tax system.

The timeline is the same as everywhere in California: unpaid taxes become defaulted after June 30, penalties build each month, and after five years a home becomes subject to the county's power to sell. Our Behind on Property Taxes page walks through every step. What is specific to Stanislaus County is how the end of that timeline works.

Stanislaus County's payment plan

Defaulted property in Stanislaus County can be redeemed in one payment or through an installment plan of redemption. The Treasurer-Tax Collector provides the current terms and the amount needed to start a plan on request at (209) 525-6388. The county's notices state that the right to start an installment plan ends on the last business day in June before a property becomes subject to the power to sell. After that date, the only way to stop a sale is to pay the entire balance.

When Stanislaus County holds its tax sale

Stanislaus County holds a public internet auction of tax-defaulted property each year on Bid4Assets. The Board of Supervisors approves the list of properties and the sale, usually at a meeting in late February, and the two most recent auctions ran for three days in May. The details are posted on the Tax Collector's Tax Sale Auction page. The right to redeem ends at 5:00 p.m. on the last business day before the auction begins. If a property sells for more than the minimum bid, parties of interest, including the former owner, can claim the excess proceeds.

If you have received a notice that your property is on the sale list, check that page and call the office the same day. Do not rely on a date someone gives you secondhand.

Inherited property in Stanislaus County

When a house is in a deceased owner's name alone, the transfer goes through the Superior Court of California, County of Stanislaus. Probate of estates and trusts is handled by the Civil and Probate Division at the City Towers Courthouse, 801 10th Street, 4th Floor, Modesto, CA 95354, where the Civil Clerk's Office and the probate courtrooms are. The clerk's office is open weekdays 8:15 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., the probate window from 8:15 a.m. to 10:00 a.m., and the court's main line is (209) 530-3100. A new courthouse is under construction downtown, but until the court announces a move, probate stays at City Towers. The court's website is stanislaus.courts.ca.gov.

Attorneys file probate documents electronically through the court's approved e-filing providers. People handling an estate without an attorney are not required to e-file and can file at the clerk's office. Since July 2025, the public can view the documents in probate files online through the court's Case Search Portal, except confidential documents. Some older, inactive cases are kept off-site and must be requested with a Research Request Form, which can take several days.

The Stanislaus County Assessor's Office is at 1010 10th Street, Suite 2400, Modesto, CA 95354, phone (209) 525-6461, open weekdays 8:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Within 150 days of a death, the person handling the estate files a Change in Ownership Statement, Death of Real Property Owner (BOE-502-D) for each property. A child or grandchild who moves into the family home files the Proposition 19 claim (BOE-19-P for parent to child, BOE-19-G for grandparent to grandchild) and must also file for the Homeowners' Exemption within one year of the transfer. The Assessor's Forms page lists each of these, and its Exclusions page explains Proposition 19.

Which court procedure applies, whether the home qualifies for the primary residence petition, and what Proposition 19 means for the tax bill are covered on our Inherited Property page.

Liens and judgments in Stanislaus County

Abstracts of judgment, federal and state tax liens, mechanic's liens, deeds of trust, and deeds are all recorded with the Stanislaus County Clerk-Recorder at 1021 I Street, Suite 101, Modesto, CA 95354. The office is open weekdays 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., and the main line is (209) 525-5250 (Spanish (209) 525-5252). Documents can be presented for recording over the counter or by mail, and documents accepted while the recording window is open, 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m., are recorded the same day.

Anyone can search the county's online index of recorded documents by grantor or grantee name, document number, recording date, Assessor's Parcel Number, or legal description. The index shows what was recorded and when, and copies are requested from the Clerk-Recorder. Documents from before 1978 are on microfilm and are found by book and page.

A name search can miss liens recorded under a misspelled or former married name, and can turn up liens against someone else with the same name. Sorting out which liens actually attach to your property, and how each one gets paid or released, is what our Liens and Judgments page covers.

Title problems in Stanislaus County

Most title problems we investigate in Stanislaus County trace back to something that was never recorded, or was recorded wrong, at the Clerk-Recorder. A loan was paid off but the reconveyance was never recorded. A house was put into a living trust on paper but never deeded into it. A deed has the wrong legal description, or still shows an owner who died. Each of these blocks a sale or a refinance, and each has a known fix, usually a corrective document, a court petition, or a release from whoever holds the old interest.

Farm and orchard land adds its own layer. Much of Stanislaus County outside the cities is agricultural, and family-held parcels are often passed down informally for decades. By the time someone wants to sell, the record owner may be a grandparent who died years ago, several heirs may each hold a share, and none of it has been recorded. Access roads, ditches, and wells that cross a neighbor's land may rest on easements that were agreed to but never recorded. Our Title Problems page explains the common fixes.

Stanislaus County questions

When does Stanislaus County hold its tax-defaulted property sale?

Once a year, as a three-day online auction on Bid4Assets. The Board of Supervisors usually approves the sale in late February, and the two most recent auctions were held in May. You can stop a sale by paying the full redemption amount before 5:00 p.m. on the last business day before the auction begins. For the current schedule, check the Treasurer-Tax Collector's Tax Sale Auction page or call (209) 525-6388.

Where do I file probate in Stanislaus County?

Estates and trusts are filed with the Superior Court of California, County of Stanislaus, at the Civil Clerk's Office in the City Towers Courthouse, 801 10th Street, 4th Floor, Modesto. Attorneys e-file through the court's approved providers, and people without an attorney can file at the clerk's office. The court's main line is (209) 530-3100.

How do I find liens recorded against my Stanislaus County property?

Search the Stanislaus County Clerk-Recorder's online index of recorded documents by owner name, parcel number, or recording date, or visit the Clerk-Recorder at 1021 I Street, Suite 101, in Modesto. The index shows what was recorded but not whether a lien was later paid, and a name search can turn up liens against someone else with the same name, which is why our free investigation checks each one against the property.
