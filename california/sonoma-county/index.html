Behind on property taxes in Sonoma County

Property taxes in Sonoma County are billed, collected, and redeemed by the Auditor-Controller-Treasurer-Tax Collector, usually shortened to ACTTC. The statewide timeline is the same here as everywhere in California: delinquent after December 10 and April 10, tax-defaulted on July 1, five years to redeem a home, then the county's power to sell. Our Behind on Property Taxes page walks through each stage. What follows is how it works in this county.

Where to go. Sonoma County Auditor-Controller-Treasurer-Tax Collector, 585 Fiscal Drive, Suite 100, Santa Rosa, CA 95403. Phone (707) 565-2281, Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. Email Taxcollector@sonomacounty.gov. The redemption amount you need to work from comes from this office.

Redemption and payment plans. The county's Redemption of Defaulted Property Taxes page explains how to pay off a defaulted balance. If the taxes are at least a year past due, you may qualify for the county's five-year installment plan. To open one, the office needs a signed Installment Plan Agreement and a first payment of at least 20 percent of the redemption amount plus a one-time $25 fee. After that, each April 10 you pay the annual installment and the current year's taxes. Miss either and the plan defaults. A new plan can only be started after July 1, and not at all once the property is subject to sale.

How Sonoma County sells tax-defaulted property. Sonoma County does not hold a tax sale every year. Recent auctions have run online through Bid4Assets, most often in the fall; the two most recent were held in November. Before each sale the county publishes the list of properties in a local newspaper once a week for three weeks and posts it on its Tax-Defaulted Property Auctions page. The right to redeem ends at 5:00 p.m. on the last business day before the sale. If a property sells for more than is owed, parties of interest, including the former owner, can claim the excess proceeds under Revenue and Taxation Code section 4675.

If you have received a notice that your property is on a list, check that page and call the ACTTC the same day. Do not rely on a date someone gives you secondhand.

Inherited property in Sonoma County

Inheriting a house here involves two county offices and, often, one court. The rules for who inherits, when probate is required, and how the new primary residence petition works are the same statewide and are covered on our Inherited Property page.

Probate court. Probate in Sonoma County is handled by the Superior Court of California, County of Sonoma. In July 2026 the court moved its probate clerk's office and probate courtroom into the new Hall of Justice at 625 Administration Drive, Santa Rosa, CA 95403, where probate is heard in Department 63 on the sixth floor. Civil and family law cases stayed at the Civil and Family Law Courthouse at 3055 Cleveland Avenue, so make sure probate papers go to Administration Drive. The court's main information line is (707) 521-6500 and the probate line is (707) 521-6650. The court's website is sonoma.courts.ca.gov.

The assessor. The Sonoma County Assessor is part of the Clerk-Recorder-Assessor department, at 585 Fiscal Drive, Room 104F, Santa Rosa, CA 95403. Real property questions: (707) 565-1888, or assessor@sonomacounty.gov. This is where the Change in Ownership Statement, Death of Real Property Owner (BOE-502-D) is filed after a death, and where a child who moves into a parent's house files the Proposition 19 parent-child claim (BOE-19-P). The county's Proposition 19 page lists the forms. Assessor staff can accept the forms but cannot tell you which ones to use or how to fill them out, which is one of the places we help.

Liens and judgments in Sonoma County

Every lien that can stop a sale or refinance in Sonoma County, from an abstract of judgment to a mechanic's lien to a state or federal tax lien, becomes attached to real property when it is recorded with the County Clerk-Recorder. How liens rank and how they get paid or negotiated is explained on our Liens and Judgments page.

Where to go. Sonoma County Clerk-Recorder, 585 Fiscal Drive, Room 103, Santa Rosa, CA 95403. Phone (707) 565-3800, Monday through Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. The office is open to the public Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, and Friday from 8:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., and Wednesday from 8:00 a.m. to 4:00 p.m. The office's Recorder Services page is at sonomacounty.gov.

How documents get recorded. Deeds, liens, releases, and abstracts of judgment can be recorded electronically, in person, by mail or courier, or through the drop box outside the office's front door. Only an original signed document can be recorded, not a photocopy.

Searching for liens yourself. The county keeps an online index of recorded documents. California law does not allow recorded documents themselves to be displayed online, so the index tells you what was recorded and when, and you request copies from the Clerk-Recorder. An index search is a good start, but it will not tell you whether a lien was later paid without a release being recorded, or whether a judgment against someone with the same name is actually against you. Sorting that out is part of the free investigation.

Title problems in Sonoma County

Most title problems come down to what is, or is not, in the Clerk-Recorder's records: a deed that was never recorded, an owner who died with no transfer on file, a legal description that does not match the parcel, a release that never got recorded. The general fixes are on our Title Problems page. Two patterns are worth naming in this county.

Fire rebuilds and transfers. Properties that burned, were rebuilt, or changed hands after the 2017 Tubbs and Nuns fires and the 2020 Glass and Walbridge fires sometimes carry complications that surface later. Permits that were never finaled, an insurance payout split between an owner and a lender, a lot sold before the rebuild, a family member who died before the claim was settled. None of these is unusual, and all of them have to be untangled before a clean sale or refinance.

Rural parcels. Much of Sonoma County outside the cities is rural: west county ridges, Russian River properties, ranch land in the north. Older parcels can depend on a road or a well that crosses a neighbor's land under an easement that was agreed to but never recorded, or recorded decades ago with a description nobody can follow today. These issues often appear only when a buyer's lender asks how the property is reached.

Sonoma County questions

When does Sonoma County hold its tax-defaulted property sale?

Not every year. Recent Sonoma County auctions have been held online through Bid4Assets, most often in the fall, with the property list published in a local newspaper and on the Auditor-Controller-Treasurer-Tax Collector's Tax-Defaulted Property Auctions page beforehand. You can stop a sale by paying the full redemption amount before 5:00 p.m. on the last business day before it starts. For the current schedule, check the county's page or call (707) 565-2281.

Where do I file probate in Sonoma County?

With the Superior Court of California, County of Sonoma. Since July 2026 the probate clerk's office and probate courtroom have been in the new Hall of Justice at 625 Administration Drive in Santa Rosa. Civil and family law matters remain at 3055 Cleveland Avenue. The probate line is (707) 521-6650.

How do I find liens recorded against my Sonoma County property?

Search the Clerk-Recorder's online index of recorded documents, or visit the Clerk-Recorder at 585 Fiscal Drive, Room 103, in Santa Rosa. The online index shows what was recorded but not the documents themselves, so you request copies from the office. Our free property investigation does this search for you and explains what each recorded item means.
