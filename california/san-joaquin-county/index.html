Behind on property taxes in San Joaquin County

Property taxes here are collected by the San Joaquin County Treasurer-Tax Collector's Office, 44 North San Joaquin Street, First Floor, Suite 150, Stockton, CA 95202. Its Redemption section handles defaulted taxes at (209) 468-2133 or tax@sjgov.org. You can look up the current year's bill online. For a redemption figure, call or email with the parcel number or address and the date you plan to pay.

The timeline is the same as everywhere in California: unpaid taxes become defaulted after June 30, redemption penalties of 1.5 percent a month start on July 1, and after five years the property becomes subject to the Tax Collector's power to sell. Our Behind on Property Taxes page walks through every step. The county's Redemption page lists the local charges.

San Joaquin County's payment plan

The county offers a five-year payment plan of redemption. To start one you pay at least 20 percent of the redemption amount and bring the current year's taxes up to date. After that you make one payment each year, due by April 10, of at least 20 percent of the redemption amount plus 1.5 percent monthly interest on the unpaid balance. Current-year taxes are paid separately, also by April 10.

Miss an annual payment or a current-year bill and the plan defaults: the county recalculates penalties from the original default date as if the plan never existed, and a new plan cannot start until the following July 1. Once five years have passed since the default, no new plan is allowed, though full redemption is still possible. The county's Redemption FAQs page has the details.

When San Joaquin County holds its tax sale

San Joaquin County sells tax-defaulted property at an online public auction on Bid4Assets. Its recent main sales have been held in March, with additional sales in some years in May or July. The right to redeem ends at 5 p.m. Pacific on the day before the sale, and a last-minute redemption has to be paid in certified funds: cash, cashier's check, or wire transfer. The current schedule is posted on the county's Auctions page.

Inherited property in San Joaquin County

When a house is in a deceased owner's name alone, the transfer goes through the Superior Court of California, County of San Joaquin. Since February 3, 2025, the court's Probate Department has been at the Lodi branch, 315 W. Elm Street, Lodi, CA 95240. The probate clerk's line is (209) 992-5696, weekdays 8 a.m. to 4 p.m. Documents can be filed at the counter, in the courthouse drop box, by mail, or through the court's e-filing system. With a hearing less than 30 days away, the court advises filing in person.

Probate cases can be looked up by party name or case number through the court's eCourt Public Portal. That matters when we need to find out whether anyone ever opened an estate.

San Joaquin combines its Assessor, Recorder, and County Clerk in one department. The Assessor's office is at 44 North San Joaquin Street, Second Floor, Suite 230, Stockton, CA 95202, at (209) 468-2630. Within 150 days of a death, the person handling the estate files a Change in Ownership Statement, Death of Real Property Owner (BOE-502-D) with the Assessor or the Recorder for each property, and in a probate case it is filed before the inventory and appraisal goes to the court. Proposition 19 parent-child claims for transfers on or after February 16, 2021 are filed through the online form linked from the Assessor's Intergenerational page, and the child has to file for the homeowners' exemption within one year of the transfer.

Which court procedure applies, whether the home qualifies for the primary residence petition, and what Proposition 19 means for the tax bill are covered on our Inherited Property page.

Liens and judgments in San Joaquin County

Abstracts of judgment, federal and state tax liens, mechanic's liens, deeds of trust, and deeds are all recorded with the Recorder-County Clerk, 44 North San Joaquin Street, Second Floor, Suite 260, Stockton, CA 95202, open weekdays 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m. The phone is (209) 468-3939. Documents can be recorded over the counter or by mail.

Anyone can search the county's Grantor/Grantee Index online through the Recorder's Self-Service Portal. The online index covers documents recorded from January 1, 1968 to the present. Older deeds and documents, back to 1851, are kept digitally and on film and have to be viewed in person at the Recorder's office. The index shows that a document exists, not the document itself. Credit reports sometimes leave out a lien release, so the recorded index is where to confirm that a paid lien was actually released.

A name search misses liens recorded under a misspelled or former married name, and can turn up liens against someone else with the same name. Sorting out which liens actually attach to your property, and how each one gets paid or released, is what our Liens and Judgments page covers.

Title problems in San Joaquin County

Most title problems we investigate in San Joaquin County trace back to something that was never recorded, or was recorded wrong, at the Recorder. A loan was paid off decades ago but the reconveyance never made it into the records. A house was put into a living trust on paper but never deeded into it. A deed has the wrong legal description or a misspelled name. An owner died, and the deed still shows them as the owner. Farm and Delta parcels divided among family members over generations can end up with legal descriptions that no longer match the ground. Each of these blocks a sale or a refinance, and each has a known fix, usually a corrective document, a court petition, or a release from whoever holds the old interest.

Because the online index starts in 1968, tracing an older chain of title usually means pulling earlier documents at the Recorder's office in Stockton. The records almost always exist. The work is reading them in order. Our Title Problems page explains the common fixes.

San Joaquin County questions

When does San Joaquin County hold its tax-defaulted property sale?

San Joaquin County sells tax-defaulted property at an online auction on Bid4Assets. Its recent main sales have been held in March, with additional sales in some years. You can stop a sale by paying the full redemption amount in certified funds before 5 p.m. Pacific on the day before it. The county posts the current schedule on its Auctions page and answers questions at (209) 468-2133.

Where do I file probate in San Joaquin County?

Probate is handled by the Superior Court of California, County of San Joaquin, at the Lodi branch, 315 W. Elm Street, Lodi, CA 95240. Documents can be filed at the counter, in the drop box, by mail, or through the court's e-filing system. The probate clerk's line is (209) 992-5696.

How do I find liens recorded against my San Joaquin County property?

Search the Recorder-County Clerk's Grantor/Grantee Index online through its Self-Service Portal. It covers documents recorded since January 1, 1968. Older records, back to 1851, have to be viewed in person at 44 North San Joaquin Street, Suite 260, Stockton. The Recorder-County Clerk answers questions at (209) 468-3939. A name search can miss liens and can turn up liens against someone else with the same name, which is why our free investigation checks each one against the property.
