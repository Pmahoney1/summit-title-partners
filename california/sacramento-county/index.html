Behind on property taxes in Sacramento County

Property taxes here are collected by the Sacramento County Department of Finance, Tax Collection and Business Licensing. The Tax Collector's public counter is at 700 H Street, Room 1710, Sacramento, CA 95814, open Monday through Friday, 8 a.m. to 5 p.m. The secured property tax line is (916) 874-6622, staffed 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. on weekdays, and you can look up a bill, a redemption amount, or a payment plan balance online through e-PropTax.

The timeline is the same as everywhere in California: unpaid taxes become defaulted after June 30, penalties build each month, and after five years a home becomes subject to the county's power to sell. Our Behind on Property Taxes page walks through every step. What is specific to Sacramento County is how the end of that timeline works.

Sacramento County's payment plan

The county offers a five-year installment plan of redemption for most prior-year secured taxes. To start one you sign a contract, pay a one-time initiation fee, and pay at least 20 percent of the original defaulted amount. After that, each installment and each new year's tax bill must be paid by April 10, or the plan defaults. You can request a contract by calling (916) 874-6622 and choosing option 0. The county's Payment Plans page has the details.

When Sacramento County holds its tax sale

Sacramento County's main tax-defaulted property auction is normally held on the last Monday in February, with a follow-up sale in May or June for property that did not sell. The auctions are held online. The right to redeem ends at the close of business on the last business day before the sale, and if a property gets no bid in February and moves to the later sale, the owner's right to redeem comes back until that sale. The Tax-Defaulted Land Unit answers questions at (916) 874-6621, and the current schedule is posted on the county's Tax Sales page.

Inherited property in Sacramento County

When a house is in a deceased owner's name alone, the transfer goes through the Sacramento Superior Court. Probate cases are handled at the William R. Ridgeway Family Relations Courthouse, 3341 Power Inn Road, Sacramento, CA 95826, which houses the court's Family Law and Probate divisions. The court's main line for probate is (916) 875-3400. Documents can be filed in person, in the drop box, by mail, or electronically through the court's approved e-filing providers. Probate cases from 2007 onward can be viewed through the court's public portal, older files back to 1937 are kept digitally at the courthouse, and records from before 1937 are held by the Center for Sacramento History. That matters when a house has not been transferred in a generation and we need to find out whether anyone ever opened an estate.

The Sacramento County Assessor's Office is at 3636 American River Drive, Suite 200, Sacramento, CA 95864. Within 150 days of a death, the person handling the estate files a Change in Ownership Statement, Death of Real Property Owner (BOE-502-D) for each property, even if the house was held in a trust. Sacramento accepts it by mail or in person, and its Loss Guidance Team answers questions at (916) 875-0750. Proposition 19 parent-child claims go to the Assessor's Property Transfer Section, at the same number, or Prop19@saccounty.gov.

Which court procedure applies, whether the home qualifies for the primary residence petition, and what Proposition 19 means for the tax bill are covered on our Inherited Property page.

Liens and judgments in Sacramento County

Abstracts of judgment, federal and state tax liens, mechanic's liens, deeds of trust, and deeds are all recorded with the Sacramento County Clerk-Recorder. The main office is at 3636 American River Drive, Suite 110, Sacramento, CA 95864, open weekdays 8 a.m. to 5 p.m., and the general line is (916) 874-6334. Documents received before 3 p.m. are recorded the same day. A satellite office at 8239 E. Stockton Boulevard, Sacramento, CA 95828 also accepts documents, but they are recorded at the main office within two business days.

Anyone can search the county's index of recorded documents online, by party name and year of recording, for documents going back to 1849. The index shows that a document exists. It does not show the document itself, so copies have to be ordered in person, by mail or fax, or by phone. When a tax lien is paid, the release is recorded and appears in the same index.

A name search finds liens against a person, which is why it misses liens recorded under a misspelled name or a former married name, and why it can turn up liens against someone else with the same name. Sorting out which liens actually attach to your property, and how each one gets paid or released, is what our Liens and Judgments page covers.

Title problems in Sacramento County

Most title problems we investigate in Sacramento County trace back to something that was never recorded, or was recorded wrong, at the Clerk-Recorder. A loan was paid off decades ago but the reconveyance never made it into the records. A house was put into a living trust on paper but never deeded into it. A deed has the wrong legal description or a misspelled name. An owner died, and the deed still shows them as the owner. Each of these blocks a sale or a refinance, and each has a known fix, usually a corrective document, a court petition, or a release from whoever holds the old interest.

Because the county's recorded index goes back to 1849 and its probate records are preserved at the courthouse and the Center for Sacramento History, the records to trace a Sacramento County chain of title almost always exist. The work is pulling them and reading them in order. Our Title Problems page explains the common fixes.

Sacramento County questions

When does Sacramento County hold its tax-defaulted property sale?

The main auction is normally held online on the last Monday in February, with a follow-up sale in May or June for property that did not sell. You can stop a sale by paying the full redemption amount before the close of business on the last business day before it. The county posts the current schedule on its Tax Sales page and answers questions at (916) 874-6621.

Where do I file probate in Sacramento County?

Probate is handled by the Sacramento Superior Court at the William R. Ridgeway Family Relations Courthouse, 3341 Power Inn Road, Sacramento, CA 95826. Documents can be filed in person, in the courthouse drop box, by mail, or through the court's approved e-filing providers. The probate line is (916) 875-3400.

How do I find liens recorded against my Sacramento County property?

Search the Sacramento County Clerk-Recorder's online index of recorded documents by owner name and year. It covers documents recorded since 1849 but does not show the documents themselves, so copies have to be ordered from the Clerk-Recorder at 3636 American River Drive, Suite 110, or by calling (916) 874-6334. A name search can miss liens and can turn up liens against someone else with the same name, which is why our free investigation checks each one against the property.
