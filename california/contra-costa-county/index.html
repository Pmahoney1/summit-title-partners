Behind on property taxes in Contra Costa County

Property taxes here are collected by the Contra Costa County Treasurer-Tax Collector in Martinez. Its Tax Bills page lists the office's current phone numbers and hours.

The timeline is the same as everywhere in California: unpaid taxes become defaulted after June 30, penalties build each month, and after five years a home becomes subject to the Tax Collector's power to sell. Each spring the county publishes a notice listing the parcels that will reach that point at the end of June if they are not redeemed or put on a payment plan. Our Behind on Property Taxes page walks through every step.

Contra Costa County's payment plan

Contra Costa County offers a five-year installment plan of redemption, which has to be started before the property becomes subject to the power to sell. The county's installment plan page has the terms.

When Contra Costa County holds its tax sale

Contra Costa County sells tax-defaulted property at an annual public auction, held online through GovEase. The right to redeem ends at the close of business on the last business day before the sale. The county posts sale details and, when available, the parcel list on its Annual Public Auction page.

Inherited property in Contra Costa County

When a house is in a deceased owner's name alone, the transfer goes through the Superior Court of California, County of Contra Costa. Probate is filed and heard at the Wakefield Taylor Courthouse, 725 Court Street, Martinez, CA 94553. For information about wills, trusts, and estates, the court lists (925) 608-2613.

Probate cases can be looked up through the court's Public Portal. That matters when we need to find out whether anyone ever opened an estate. For estates from the county's early years, the Contra Costa County Historical Society in Martinez holds old county court records, including probate files, and keeps an online index of old probate cases by the name of the person who died.

The Contra Costa County Assessor's office is also in Martinez. It handles the property tax side of a death, including the change in ownership. For Proposition 19 transfers between parents and children, the Assessor's Proposition 19 page refers readers to the State Board of Equalization.

Which court procedure applies, whether the home qualifies for the primary residence petition, and what Proposition 19 means for the tax bill are covered on our Inherited Property page.

Liens and judgments in Contra Costa County

Abstracts of judgment, federal and state tax liens, mechanic's liens, deeds of trust, and deeds are recorded with the Contra Costa County Clerk-Recorder in Martinez. Its Recorder pages explain how to search the index of recorded documents and order copies, and list the office's phone number.

The online index shows that a document exists, not the document itself, and it does not reach all the way back. Older records can be searched in person at the Recorder's office in Martinez. Credit reports sometimes leave out a lien release, so the recorded index is where to confirm that a paid lien was actually released.

A name search misses liens recorded under a misspelled or former married name, and can turn up liens against someone else with the same name. Sorting out which liens actually attach to your property, and how each one gets paid or released, is what our Liens and Judgments page covers.

Title problems in Contra Costa County

Most title problems we investigate in Contra Costa County trace back to something that was never recorded, or was recorded wrong. A loan was paid off decades ago but the reconveyance never made it into the records. A house was put into a living trust on paper but never deeded into it. A deed has the wrong legal description or a misspelled name. An owner died, and the deed still shows them as the owner. Each of these blocks a sale or a refinance, and each has a known fix, usually a corrective document, a court petition, or a release from whoever holds the old interest.

In East County, much of the Delta around Bethel Island, Discovery Bay, and Byron sits on leveed tracts and islands inside reclamation and improvement districts, which can add their own assessments to a property's history. Our Title Problems page explains the common fixes.

Contra Costa County questions

When does Contra Costa County hold its tax-defaulted property sale?

Contra Costa County holds an annual public auction of tax-defaulted property, held online through GovEase. You can stop a sale by redeeming before the close of business on the last business day before it. The county posts sale details on its Annual Public Auction page.

Where do I file probate in Contra Costa County?

All probate documents are filed and heard at the Wakefield Taylor Courthouse, 725 Court Street, Martinez, CA 94553. For information about wills, trusts, and estates, the court lists (925) 608-2613.

How do I find liens recorded against my Contra Costa County property?

Search the Clerk-Recorder's online index of recorded documents, linked from its Recorder pages. Older records can be searched in person at the Recorder's office in Martinez. A name search can miss liens and can turn up liens against someone else with the same name, which is why our free investigation checks each one against the property.
