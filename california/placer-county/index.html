Behind on property taxes in Placer County

Property taxes here are collected by the Placer County Treasurer-Tax Collector, 2976 Richardson Drive, Auburn, CA 95603. Defaulted taxes are handled by its Redemption Section at 530-889-4109. The county's Tax Defaulted Bills and Payment Plan page explains defaulted bills and the payment plan.

If "Tax Defaulted" is printed on your bill, the Tax Collector shows unpaid taxes from a prior year. The timeline is the same as everywhere in California: unpaid taxes become defaulted after June 30, penalties build each month, and after five years a home becomes subject to the county's power to sell at public auction. The county publishes its annual list of tax-defaulted property each September. Our Behind on Property Taxes page walks through every step.

Placer County's payment plan

Placer County offers a five-year payment plan, also called an installment plan of redemption. A plan cannot be started once the property is subject to the power to sell, and the balance can be paid off at any time. Questions about payment plans go to 530-889-4120.

When Placer County holds its tax sale

Placer County sells tax-defaulted property at a public auction. The county does not sell tax lien certificates. The next sale is tentatively scheduled for fall 2027, with the date and property list expected in summer 2027. The right to redeem ends at the close of business on the last business day before the sale. The county's Tax Land Sale page posts the schedule.

Inherited property in Placer County

When a house is in a deceased owner's name alone, the transfer goes through the Superior Court of California, County of Placer. Its clerk's offices are at the Hon. Howard G. Gibson Courthouse, 10820 Justice Center Drive, Roseville, CA 95678, and the Historic Courthouse, 101 Maple Street, Auburn, CA 95603. The Civil and Probate line is (916) 408-6000, option 5.

The Placer County Assessor handles the property tax side of a death, including the change in ownership; the county's Change in Ownership page has the details. Proposition 19 rules for transfers from parents to children are explained by the State Board of Equalization.

Placer has large age-restricted communities, including Sun City Roseville and Sun City Lincoln Hills. When a home there is inherited by children who are too young to live in it or have no plans to move in, the family often has to decide quickly whether to keep, rent, or sell, and Proposition 19 affects that choice. Our Inherited Property page covers which court procedure applies, the primary residence petition, and what Proposition 19 means for the tax bill.

Liens and judgments in Placer County

Abstracts of judgment, federal and state tax liens, mechanic's liens, deeds of trust, and deeds are recorded with the Placer County Clerk-Recorder. Its Real Property Records page explains how to search the county's recorded index by name and order copies, and lists the office's current locations, hours, and phone number.

The index shows that a document exists, not the document itself; copies are ordered from the Clerk-Recorder. Credit reports sometimes leave out a lien release, so the recorded index is where to confirm that a paid lien was actually released.

A name search misses liens recorded under a misspelled or former married name, and can turn up liens against someone else with the same name. Sorting out which liens actually attach to your property, and how each one gets paid or released, is what our Liens and Judgments page covers.

Title problems in Placer County

Most title problems we investigate in Placer County trace back to something that was never recorded, or was recorded wrong. A loan was paid off decades ago but the reconveyance never made it into the records. A house was put into a living trust on paper but never deeded into it. A deed has the wrong legal description or a misspelled name. An owner died, and the deed still shows them as the owner. Each of these blocks a sale or a refinance, and each has a known fix, usually a corrective document, a court petition, or a release from whoever holds the old interest.

In the foothills, ownership history can run back to the Gold Rush. The Placer County Archives holds the county's mining claim records from 1850 on, some older than the county itself, and rural foothill parcels can carry legal descriptions written long before modern surveys. Tracing that kind of chain of title is slow, but the records almost always exist. Our Title Problems page explains the common fixes.

Placer County questions

When does Placer County hold its tax-defaulted property sale?

Placer County holds a public auction of tax-defaulted property, and the next sale is tentatively scheduled for fall 2027. You can stop a sale by redeeming before the close of business on the last business day before it. The county posts the schedule on its Tax Land Sale page, and the Redemption Section answers questions at 530-889-4109.

Where do I file probate in Placer County?

Probate is handled by the Placer County Superior Court. Its clerk's offices are at the Gibson Courthouse, 10820 Justice Center Drive, Roseville, and the Historic Courthouse, 101 Maple Street, Auburn. The Civil and Probate line is (916) 408-6000, option 5.

How do I find liens recorded against my Placer County property?

Search the Clerk-Recorder's recorded index by name, linked from the county's Real Property Records page. A name search can miss liens and can turn up liens against someone else with the same name, which is why our free investigation checks each one against the property.
