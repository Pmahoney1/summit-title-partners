More Than One Way Out: Keep, Stay, Sell, or Exit Options for California Homeowners

Not every situation calls for a sale. Depending on your needs, the path may involve keeping the property, curing the issue, selling with time to relocate, a creative structure, or a direct purchase.

Below is each path, when it fits, and when it does not. Which one is right for you comes out of the free property investigation, not a sales pitch. Keep or stay in your home

For many owners the goal is simply to stay. When the problem is back property taxes, the most common tool is the five-year installment plan of redemption for tax-defaulted property (Revenue and Taxation Code sections 4216 through 4219). You pay 20 percent or more of the redemption amount plus a fee to open it, then at least 20 percent of the balance each year by April 10, with interest, while keeping new taxes current.

Owners who are 62 or older, blind, or disabled, live in the home, hold at least 40 percent equity, and fall under the annual income limit may qualify for the State Controller's Property Tax Postponement Program, which pays current-year taxes and is repaid when the home is sold, refinanced, or transferred. Paying in full or a private tax relief loan are other routes. We do not lend.

When it fits

The payments fit your income, the property is worth keeping, and the plan opens before the county gains the power to sell.

When it doesn't

The property is already subject to the power to sell, or the total cost of keeping the house is more than the household can carry.

Cure the problem and hold

Sometimes the house is not the problem; something attached to it is. A clouded title, a judgment lien, or an unfinished probate blocks a refinance or sale. Fix that, and the owner can keep, refinance, or sell later. Curing can mean correcting a recording error or a break in the chain of title, paying or negotiating liens and judgments, or finishing the probate or petition that puts the heirs on title. When an attorney is needed, we bring one in.

When it fits

The property has real value, the owner wants to hold it, and the problem has a known fix.

When it doesn't

The cure costs more than the property is worth to you, or a tax sale arrives first. Then the cure happens inside a sale.

Sell on your timeline

If keeping the property is not the goal, a conventional sale through escrow is usually the cleanest exit. Taxes, liens, and the mortgage are paid from proceeds at closing, and you keep the rest. It need not be rushed. It can have a long close, a move-out date that gives you time to relocate, or a rent-back that lets you stay after closing for an agreed period as a tenant.

When it fits

The title can be insured, there is equity, and the owner wants the open market's best price. Where it works, it is usually what we recommend.

When it doesn't

The property cannot be financed or insured as it stands, or a deadline leaves no room for a listing.

A creative structure

Some situations fit neither a straight keep nor a straight sale. Examples include seller-carry terms, where the owner finances part of the price and is paid over time; a partial sale or buyout, where one co-owner or heir buys out the others; and lease arrangements, such as a leaseback or a lease with an option to buy. These carry legal and tax consequences that depend on your situation. We do not give legal or tax advice, and we recommend you review any structure with your own attorney and tax advisor before signing.

When it fits

Co-owners disagree, an owner needs income more than a lump sum, or an owner needs to stay while ownership changes.

When it doesn't

A plain sale or a payment plan gets the same result more simply.

A direct purchase by Summit Title Partners

Summit Title Partners can buy the property directly, as-is, with the problem attached, and the owner chooses the closing and move-out dates. We may earn from that purchase, and we say so before you decide.

When it fits

A title defect, stacked liens, a missing heir, or heavy repairs keep the property off the open market, and the owner values certainty and timing over the last dollar.

When it doesn't

A conventional sale would net you more. Then we will tell you to sell conventionally.

Common questions

What are my options if I am behind on property taxes in California?

Pay in full, open a five-year installment plan, apply for Property Tax Postponement if eligible, take a tax relief loan, sell with time to relocate, or use a creative structure or direct purchase. Which are open depends on where the property is on the county's timeline.

Can I keep my house if I owe back taxes in California?

Usually, if you act before the right of redemption ends. An installment plan spreads defaulted taxes over up to five years. Once the property is subject to the power to sell, only payment in full keeps it.

What is a rent-back?

You sell, then stay for an agreed period after closing, paying rent to the new owner. You get the proceeds and time to move.

Do I have to sell to fix a title or lien problem?

No. Many can be cured while you keep the property, then you can keep, refinance, or sell later.

Will Summit Title Partners always offer to buy my property?

No. If a conventional sale, a payment plan, or a cure serves you better, the written summary says so.
