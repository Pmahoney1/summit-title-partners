Inherited a House in California? What to Do Next

A parent, a grandparent, a sibling, or a spouse has died and left a house in California. Maybe there was a will, maybe a trust, maybe nothing. The property tax bills keep arriving in their name. The mortgage statement, if there is one, does too. A relative wants to sell. Another wants to keep it. Someone mentioned probate and nobody is sure whether it applies.

The first thing to know is that in California, who legally owns an inherited house depends far more on how the title was held than on what anyone said or wrote. The second is that there are several ways to transfer a house after a death, and most of them are faster and cheaper than full probate. The third is that the deadlines on the house itself, especially property taxes, do not pause while the family works it out.

How inheriting a house works in California

Start with how the title was held

The deed on file with the county recorder decides the path. There are five common situations.

Joint tenancy with right of survivorship, or community property with right of survivorship. The surviving owner already owns the whole property. No probate. The survivor records an affidavit of death with a certified death certificate, and the title is clean.

A living trust. If the house was deeded into the trust, the successor trustee controls it under the trust's terms. No probate. The trustee records an affidavit of death of trustee and can then sell or distribute the property. The common problem here is a trust that was signed but a house that was never actually deeded into it, which usually requires a petition under Probate Code section 850 to fix.

A revocable transfer on death deed. If the owner recorded one before death, the named beneficiary takes the property by recording an affidavit of death and giving the required notice to the owner's heirs (Probate Code sections 5600 and following). No probate, but the beneficiary takes the property subject to any liens and to creditor claims.

The decedent's name alone, with a will. The will controls who inherits, but it does not transfer the house by itself. One of the court procedures below is required.

The decedent's name alone, with no will. The house passes under California's intestate succession rules, and one of the court procedures below is required.

When full probate is required, and the shortcuts

Full probate is a court-supervised administration under Probate Code section 8000 and following. In most counties it runs nine to eighteen months from filing to final distribution, and attorney and executor fees are set by statute as a percentage of the gross estate value, not the equity (Probate Code section 10810). A house with a large mortgage still generates fees on its full value.

California offers three shortcuts for smaller estates, and since 2025 one of them covers most family homes.

Petition to determine succession to real property. For deaths on or after April 1, 2025, a decedent's primary residence worth up to $750,000 can be transferred to the heirs by a petition under Probate Code section 13151, without a full probate, as long as the rest of the estate is under the small estate limit. This came from Assembly Bill 2016 and is the procedure most inherited California homes now qualify for. It still goes through the probate court, but it is a single petition and hearing rather than a year of administration, and it can be filed 40 days after death.

Affidavit for real property of small value. If all the decedent's California real property is worth $69,625 or less, which is rare for a house but common for a vacant lot or a fractional interest, it can be transferred by an affidavit filed with the court and recorded, six months after death (Probate Code section 13200).

Small estate affidavit for personal property. For deaths on or after April 1, 2025, personal property up to $208,850 passes by affidavit under Probate Code section 13100. This does not transfer real estate, but it matters because the house's value is excluded from the calculation when the primary residence petition is used. The Judicial Council adjusts these dollar limits every three years.

A surviving spouse also has a separate spousal property petition under Probate Code section 13650 that transfers community and separate property to the spouse without full administration.

No will: who inherits under California law

If the decedent left no will and no trust, Probate Code sections 6401 and 6402 decide who inherits. The surviving spouse takes the decedent's half of the community property, which means a spouse who co-owned the family home as community property usually ends up with all of it. Separate property is split between the spouse and the children depending on how many children there are. If there is no spouse, the children inherit equally. If there are no children, it goes to the decedent's parents, then siblings, then more distant relatives.

Two practical problems come up constantly. The first is heirs nobody can find: a child from a first marriage, a sibling who moved away decades ago, a grandchild who inherits a deceased parent's share. Every one of them owns a piece of the house, and no one can sell it without them. The second is a house that was never probated at all, sometimes for a generation, so the heirs of heirs now own fractions of it and the title shows an owner who died in the 1990s. Both are solvable, and both take research. That research is the core of what we do.

Property taxes after a death

The county is not notified of a death automatically. The person handling the estate must file a Change in Ownership Statement, Death of Real Property Owner (form BOE-502-D) with the county assessor within 150 days of the death. Missing it brings a penalty and does not prevent reassessment.

Reassessment is the bigger issue. Since Proposition 19 took effect in February 2021, a house passing from parent to child keeps its old property tax assessment only if the child moves in and makes it a principal residence, files a homeowners' exemption claim within one year of the transfer, and the home's market value is within the parent's taxable value plus a limit that started at $1 million and is adjusted every two years. If the child rents the house out, keeps it as a second home, or lets it sit, the assessor reassesses it to market value as of the date of death and the tax bill can multiply. The claim is filed with the county assessor on form BOE-19-P.

Meanwhile the regular tax bills keep coming due on the same schedule as always, November 1 and February 1, and go delinquent and then tax-defaulted on the same timeline whether or not anyone is opening the mail. Our Behind on Property Taxes page covers that timeline, including the five-year rule that applies to a deceased owner's house exactly as it would to anyone else's.

The mortgage, the liens, and the name on the deed

If there is a mortgage, federal law lets a relative who inherits and occupies the home keep making the payments without the lender calling the loan due. A reverse mortgage is different: it becomes due when the borrower dies, and the heirs typically have a limited window to pay it off, sell, or sign the house over. Liens, judgments, and unpaid taxes attached to the decedent stay attached to the house. Our Liens and Judgments page explains how those get paid or cleared.

Then there is the deed itself. Until one of the procedures above is completed and recorded, the county's records show the deceased person as the owner. No title company will insure a sale and no lender will lend. Years later this is the most common title problem we see on inherited homes, and it is covered on our Title Problems page.

Your realistic options

Which of these are open depends on how the title was held, whether the estate has been administered, who the heirs are, and what is attached to the house. That is what the investigation establishes. Several of these do not involve us at all.

Keep the house as your home

If you qualify for the Proposition 19 exclusion, move in and file the homeowners' exemption claim within a year, and the property tax stays where it was. You will still need to complete the transfer procedure so the deed is in your name, and you will need to keep any mortgage current. If other heirs own a share, you buy them out or agree on terms, and that agreement should be in writing and recorded.

Keep it as a rental

Legal and often sensible, but expect reassessment to market value, because Proposition 19 does not protect a rental. Run the numbers with the new tax bill, not the old one. Every heir on title shares in the income and the decisions, so an operating agreement between co-owners avoids the dispute that usually follows.

Sell after the transfer is complete

The cleanest sale happens after the petition, affidavit, or probate has put the heirs on title. The house sells through escrow like any other, the mortgage and liens are paid from proceeds, and each heir receives their share. For income tax purposes inherited property generally takes a stepped-up basis at the date-of-death value, which often means little or no capital gain on a prompt sale. Confirm that with a tax advisor.

Sell during probate

A personal representative with full authority under the Independent Administration of Estates Act can sell the house during probate with notice to the heirs and without a court hearing. With limited authority, the sale needs court confirmation, which adds time and allows overbidding at the hearing. Either way, a sale can close before the estate is finished.

One heir buys out the others

Common when one sibling wants the house and the rest want cash. It requires a current value everyone accepts, a source of funds for the buyer, and a deed from each selling heir. Where the family cannot agree, any co-owner can file a partition action, and since 2023 California's Partition of Real Property Act gives the heirs who want to keep the home the first right to buy out the ones who want to sell at an appraised value, before the court orders a sale.

A structured sale or a direct purchase

When the house needs work, carries liens, has a missing heir, or the family needs time, a conventional listing may not fit. The alternatives, including a sale with a long close, a sale with time to clear out the house, or a direct purchase by Summit Title Partners, are on our Flexible Solutions page. We may earn from a transaction we join, and we say so before you choose. We will not propose one if a plain sale through escrow serves you better.

Doing nothing

Also an option, and the most expensive one. The property taxes go delinquent, then tax-defaulted, then the county gains the power to sell. The reassessment penalty accrues. Heirs die and their shares split further. Someone eventually tries to sell and discovers the house still belongs, on paper, to someone who died fifteen years ago. Almost every inherited house we investigate that has become a hard problem became one because nobody acted in the first year.

Common questions

Do I have to go through probate to sell an inherited house in California?

Not always. If the house was in a trust, held in joint tenancy, or covered by a transfer on death deed, no probate is needed. If it was in the decedent's name alone, you need a court procedure, but for deaths on or after April 1, 2025 a primary residence worth up to $750,000 can be transferred by a single petition under Probate Code section 13151 instead of full probate.

How long does probate take in California?

Full probate usually takes nine to eighteen months from filing to distribution, longer if there are disputes, missing heirs, or creditor claims. The primary residence petition and the small estate affidavits are measured in weeks to a few months.

Who inherits a house in California if there is no will?

Probate Code sections 6401 and 6402 decide. The surviving spouse takes the decedent's share of community property. Separate property is divided between the spouse and children. With no spouse, the children inherit equally, then parents, then siblings. Every heir owns a share, and every share has to be accounted for before the house can be sold.

Will my property taxes go up when I inherit my parents' house?

Under Proposition 19, only if you do not make it your principal residence. If you move in and file the homeowners' exemption within a year of the transfer, the old assessment carries over, up to a value limit. If you rent it out or keep it as a second home, the assessor reassesses it to market value as of the date of death.

One of the heirs cannot be found. Can we still sell?

Not until their interest is dealt with. Options include an heir search to locate them, a probate court order addressing the missing heir's share, or in some cases a partition action. Locating missing heirs is a regular part of our investigation.

The owner died years ago and nothing was ever filed. Is it too late?

No. A late probate or petition can still be filed, and the heirs of any heir who has since died step into their share. It takes more research, because the list of owners has grown, but these estates get resolved regularly.

There is a reverse mortgage on the house. What happens?

A reverse mortgage becomes due when the borrower dies. The lender gives the heirs a window, usually with extensions available, to pay the balance, sell the house, or sign it over. If the house is worth more than the loan, the heirs keep the difference from a sale, so acting quickly protects that equity.
